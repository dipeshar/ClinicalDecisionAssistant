# Surgical risk stratification for valve and coronary procedures (synthetic summary)

## SURG-KB-01

A composite predicted mortality risk of 4% to 8% on validated cardiac surgical risk models is generally classified as high-intermediate risk. Patients in this band are not routinely excluded from surgery, but the risk-benefit balance depends heavily on modifiable factors such as renal function, frailty, and respiratory reserve, and typically warrants multidisciplinary heart-team review before a final decision.

## SURG-KB-02

Cardiopulmonary bypass exposes the kidneys to reduced perfusion pressure and a systemic inflammatory response, both of which can precipitate acute kidney injury. Baseline chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and anaemia each independently raise this risk, and their combination is additive rather than simply cumulative, meaning a patient with all three faces materially higher risk than any single factor alone would suggest.

## SURG-KB-03

Frailty, assessed by scales such as the Clinical Frailty Scale, gait speed, and grip strength, predicts prolonged recovery, higher complication rates, and reduced tolerance of major cardiac surgery independent of chronological age. A patient scoring in the vulnerable-to-mildly-frail range is not automatically unsuitable for surgery, but the finding should prompt consideration of prehabilitation and closer post-operative monitoring.

## SURG-KB-04

Moderate chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with FEV1 in the 50 to 60 percent predicted range is associated with a measurably higher risk of prolonged ventilation and pulmonary complications after sternotomy and cardiopulmonary bypass, compared with normal lung function. This risk is generally considered manageable with pre-operative optimisation and is not, on its own, a reason to decline surgery.
