# Aortic valve intervention options and coronary revascularisation strategy (synthetic summary)

## SURG-KB-05

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation is generally preferred over surgical replacement in patients at high or prohibitive surgical risk, and is increasingly used in intermediate-risk patients depending on anatomical suitability. It avoids cardiopulmonary bypass and sternotomy, which reduces peri-operative renal and pulmonary stress, but pre-procedural planning typically requires contrast-enhanced CT imaging for annulus sizing.

## SURG-KB-06

A documented moderate reaction to iodinated contrast, including urticaria and bronchospasm, does not absolutely preclude contrast-enhanced imaging, but requires a premedication protocol and should prompt consideration of whether the imaging is strictly necessary or whether an alternative, lower-contrast protocol can achieve the same planning information.

## SURG-KB-07

When severe aortic stenosis coexists with significant proximal coronary disease, concurrent surgical treatment of both lesions in a single operation is generally preferred over a staged approach, because it avoids a second procedure and its associated risk, provided the patient's overall risk profile tolerates the combined operation.

## SURG-KB-08

An aortic valve area below 1.0 cm squared with a mean gradient above 40 mmHg defines severe aortic stenosis. Once a patient with severe stenosis becomes symptomatic, expert consensus favours intervention over continued observation, since symptom onset itself marks a significant worsening in prognosis regardless of the specific values recorded.
