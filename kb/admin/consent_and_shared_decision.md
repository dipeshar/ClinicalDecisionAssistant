# Informed consent and shared decision-making standards (synthetic summary)

## ADMIN-KB-01

The standard of informed consent should be proportional to the risk of the procedure being proposed. For a procedure carrying a predicted mortality risk in the high-intermediate range, consent documentation should explicitly address that risk in terms the patient can understand, alongside the alternatives considered and why each was or was not recommended.

## ADMIN-KB-02

When a patient has expressed a specific fear about a particular outcome — for example, a fear of losing independence, or of a specific complication associated with the procedure — that concern should be explicitly documented as having been discussed as part of the consent process, including what steps are being taken to address it, rather than only being noted informally.

## ADMIN-KB-03

Family members raising questions about a proposed procedure, including asking about less invasive alternatives, should have those questions addressed and the answers documented as part of the shared decision-making record, particularly when a family member is the patient's primary support and contact.

## ADMIN-KB-04

Shared decision-making is not satisfied merely by a patient agreeing to proceed; it requires that the patient's own stated goals and concerns be weighed explicitly against the clinical recommendation, with any tension between the two acknowledged in the documented consent conversation rather than left unaddressed.
