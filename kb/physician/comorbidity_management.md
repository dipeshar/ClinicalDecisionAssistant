# Interacting comorbidities in cardiac risk assessment (synthetic summary)

## PHYS-KB-05

Long-standing type 2 diabetes with suboptimal glycaemic control is a recognised contributor to progressive kidney disease, independent of any acute insult. In a patient with both conditions, declining renal function should generally be interpreted as at least partly diabetes-driven rather than attributed solely to a single acute event.

## PHYS-KB-06

Anaemia in the setting of chronic kidney disease is common and typically multifactorial, reflecting reduced erythropoietin production alongside other contributing factors such as iron status. It reduces the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity, which becomes clinically relevant during physiological stress such as major surgery, where oxygen demand rises sharply.

## PHYS-KB-07

There is no established medical therapy that reverses severe calcific aortic stenosis; guideline-directed medical management in this setting is limited to treating associated conditions, such as hypertension or heart failure symptoms, rather than the valve lesion itself. Definitive treatment requires either surgical or transcatheter valve intervention.

## PHYS-KB-08

Mild-to-moderate reduction in left ventricular ejection fraction, in the setting of severe aortic stenosis, often reflects the heart's response to a chronically increased afterload rather than a separate, independent cardiomyopathy. Ejection fraction in this range frequently improves after the valve obstruction is relieved, which is a relevant consideration when weighing intervention against continued observation.
