# Natural history and risk markers of severe aortic stenosis (synthetic summary)

## PHYS-KB-01

Once severe aortic stenosis becomes symptomatic, average survival without intervention is commonly cited as under two years, with syncope and heart failure symptoms both independently associated with a steeper decline. Medical therapy alone does not meaningfully alter this trajectory, since no medication reverses the mechanical valve obstruction driving the disease.

## PHYS-KB-02

A syncopal episode in the setting of severe aortic stenosis is considered a high-risk marker, reflecting the heart's reduced ability to increase output during exertion. Its occurrence typically accelerates the timeline for considering intervention, since the risk of a subsequent, potentially fatal event rises once syncope has occurred.

## PHYS-KB-03

NT-proBNP is a marker of cardiac wall stress and rises as the heart works harder against a narrowed valve. A markedly elevated level in a patient with severe aortic stenosis, well above the normal reference range, supports a diagnosis of significant haemodynamic burden and is generally taken as corroborating evidence for a symptomatic, high-risk disease state.

## PHYS-KB-04

NYHA functional class III indicates symptoms with less-than-ordinary activity, such as breathlessness after walking a short distance or climbing one flight of stairs. This class of limitation, in the context of a structural cardiac lesion, is generally regarded as a threshold beyond which conservative management alone is unlikely to be adequate.
