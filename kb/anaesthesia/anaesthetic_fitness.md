# Anaesthetic fitness assessment for major cardiac surgery (synthetic summary)

## ANAES-KB-01

ASA physical status class IV describes a patient with severe systemic disease that is a constant threat to life. Multiple concurrent organ-system impairments — for example, significant renal impairment combined with a major cardiac or respiratory condition — commonly place a patient at or near this level, reflecting significant but not automatically prohibitive anaesthetic risk. The classification reflects cumulative burden across systems, not any single diagnosis alone.

## ANAES-KB-02

Moderate COPD with reduced FEV1 increases the risk of prolonged mechanical ventilation and post-operative pulmonary complications after sternotomy and cardiopulmonary bypass. Pre-operative optimisation of bronchodilator therapy and a clear extubation plan are standard mitigations, and this risk alone is not generally considered a contraindication to proceeding with major cardiac surgery.

## ANAES-KB-03

Frailty is an independent predictor of post-operative delirium, particularly after procedures involving cardiopulmonary bypass and general anaesthesia in older patients. A patient with a borderline frailty score should be flagged for delirium-prevention protocols, including early mobilisation and careful medication review, rather than treated as an automatic exclusion from surgery.

## ANAES-KB-04

Peri-operative risk assessment should weigh cumulative comorbidity burden rather than any single factor in isolation. A patient with three or more moderate-severity comorbidities, such as chronic kidney disease, diabetes, and respiratory disease together, generally carries materially higher anaesthetic risk than the sum of each condition's individually reported risk would suggest.
