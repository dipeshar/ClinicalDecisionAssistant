# Peri-operative renal protection and dialysis readiness (synthetic summary)

## ANAES-KB-05

Patients with chronic kidney disease stage 3b or worse undergoing cardiopulmonary bypass should have a documented renal-protection plan, including minimising bypass time where feasible, avoiding nephrotoxic agents peri-operatively, and close post-operative monitoring of creatinine and urine output for the first 48 to 72 hours.

## ANAES-KB-06

When the predicted risk of post-operative acute kidney injury is high, contingency planning should explicitly address dialysis readiness, including confirming access to renal replacement therapy and involving nephrology in the peri-operative period, rather than only after injury has already occurred.

## ANAES-KB-07

Pre-operative anaemia in a patient facing major cardiac surgery warrants a documented plan for blood management, since surgery of this kind carries a meaningful risk of further blood loss on top of an already reduced starting haemoglobin, and inadequate oxygen delivery peri-operatively can itself contribute to organ injury, including the kidneys.

## ANAES-KB-08

Intensive care recovery after combined valve replacement and coronary bypass surgery typically involves a longer initial stay than after a transcatheter valve procedure, reflecting the greater physiological insult of cardiopulmonary bypass and sternotomy. This difference in expected recovery burden is a legitimate factor when weighing procedural options for a frail patient.
